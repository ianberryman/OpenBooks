# OpenBooks Contributor License Agreement

**Version 1.0**

This document contains two agreements:

- The **Individual Contributor License Agreement (ICLA)** — every contributor signs this.
- The **Corporate Contributor License Agreement (CCLA)** — additionally required when a company
  owns the rights to your work, for example because you contribute as part of your employment.

Both are adapted from the Apache Software Foundation's Individual and Corporate Contributor License
Agreements, with two OpenBooks-specific additions: an explicit outbound-licensing and relicensing
clause (ICLA section 4), and an assignment clause (ICLA section 10).

Thank you for your interest in OpenBooks. Please read the whole document before signing, and keep a
copy for your records.

## Why a CLA and not a DCO

OpenBooks is dual-licensed: the server is AGPL-3.0 and the contract packages are Apache-2.0 with a
linking exception (see [NOTICE](NOTICE)). Moving code between those licenses, publishing
`@openbooks/plugin-api` under Apache-2.0, and offering commercial licensing all require the project
to hold rights broad enough to license contributed code under terms other than the one it arrived
under. A Developer Certificate of Origin certifies provenance but grants no such rights, so it is
not sufficient here.

This agreement is as much for your protection as the project's. It is non-exclusive: you keep your
copyright, and you keep the right to use your own contribution for anything else you like.

## How to sign

1. Read this document in full.
2. Email a statement of agreement to **ian@ach-pro.com** with the subject
   `OpenBooks CLA — <your name>`, including every starred field from the signature block below and
   the sentence: _"I have read the OpenBooks Contributor License Agreement version 1.0 and I agree
   to it."_ Attaching a signed PDF or image of the completed signature block is welcome but not
   required; an unambiguous email from the address you commit with is sufficient.
3. In the description of your first pull request, state: `CLA signed: <name> <email>`. A reviewer
   confirms against the signed agreements on file before merging.

Signing is one-time and covers all of your future contributions. If your employer needs to sign the
CCLA as well, both must be on file before your first contribution is merged.

There is no CLA bot yet — the check is manual. Automating it is tracked for the launch-readiness
milestone; see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).
